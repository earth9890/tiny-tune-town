## 3. Sensitivity rules
Islam: never depict Allah or any prophet (incl. Muhammad) or angels with faces; mascot ok but do not put Ollie in prayer/sujood/Kaaba scenes or giving "teaching"; Arabic spelling/tashkeel exact; Arabic text right-to-left correct and not cropped; avoid mispronounced Allah's names. Music: scholarly mainstream (IslamQA, SeekersGuidance, Darul Ifta Birmingham) treats instruments as impermissible except duff; vocal-only nasheed is widely accepted; synth beats imitating instruments contested. So: offer vocals-only (or minimal) version, label it "Vocals only / No music" (it is a proven hook, Aunty Em 3.2M). Names list: use the Tirmidhi (3507)/al-Walid ibn Muslim list, the standard printed one, but note scholars call that hadith gharib/weak and the enumeration disputed (some count 98, "Al-Ahad" added by Ibn Khuzaima; Ibn Hazm, Ibn Hajar variants). Use the common list and mention "the commonly recited list" in description. Don't claim "99 names guarantee Paradise"-style promises as fact beyond the hadith wording.
Hindu: don't make deities silly, no comedic mishaps with gods, no feet-level/ground placement of deity images, no mixing with food/toilet/weird props, standard iconography (Ganesha with modak, mouse; Krishna with flute), don't merge Hindu and Muslim scenes in one video, correct Sanskrit/Devanagari, keep mantras whole (don't cut/loop mid-syllable weirdly), avoid sexualised/skin-lightened art; mala/tilak on kid ok, no caricature. Mascot: octopus is probably fine as a viewer/guide but keep it OUT of the scene of sacred things (no octopus wearing a kufi, no octopus praying, no octopus next to deities); safest is a human kid cartoon as the lead and Ollie only in intro/outro. Judgement call, not verified by a source.
Same vs separate channel: no data collected on cross-religion retention. Reasoning: faith audiences search by topic and subscribe by community; mixed lists make the algorithm blur the audience and parents see Islamic videos next to Hindu ones in recommendations; successful channels observed are single-tradition (Iqra Kids, Muslim Reminder Kids = Islamic only; Bal Bhakti, Om Namah Shivaya Kids, MythoKids = Hindu only; Shemaroo is multi-genre but run by a media company). **Recommendation: separate channels (or at least separate channel per tradition), keep Tiny Tune Town generic.** Low-risk alternative: one test video on the main channel, move if it works.
YouTube Made-for-Kids: I did not find a specific religious-content rule in the web search (the search returned only Islamic rulings). General MFK facts to check in YouTube Help: content directed at children must be marked MFK (comments/personalized ads off), COPPA; no policy bans religious content for kids, but standard kids-content quality rules (no misleading, no harmful content) apply. Verify at support.google.com/youtube (not done here).

## 4. Feasibility with ACE-Step AI singer
Risk: high for Arabic (emphatic consonants ص ض ط ظ, ع ح خ غ ق, long vowels) and Sanskrit (aspirates, retroflex). Plan: (1) write lyrics in phonetic English-syllable spelling (e.g. "Ar-Rah-maan, Ar-Ra-heem") and test 1-2 clips only (paid-test rule); (2) one-name-per-line, slow, ~2-4 syllables, repetitive hook so errors are obvious; (3) transcribe the sung output with Whisper (Arabic model) and compare to the target; (4) get the owner or a native speaker (Arabic: a Muslim friend/imam; Sanskrit: pandit) to listen to every name before publishing; (5) put Arabic script + transliteration + English meaning on screen; (6) fallback: use a native-speaker human recitation or a spoken-word layer for the names and use AI only for the English refrain; (7) if a name is bad, re-seed rather than patch. Consider starting with a shorter set (e.g. 10 names or Ganesha 12 names) to prove pronunciation quality before a full 99/108.
