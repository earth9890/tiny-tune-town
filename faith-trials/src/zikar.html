Dhikr · Asmaul Husna

99 Names of Allah — complete list with Arabic and meaning

The Zikar Team · 10 min read · Published 23 Jun 2026

The 99 Names of Allah — Asmaul Husna (الأسماء الحسنى), the Beautiful Names — are the attributes and epithets by which Allah has described Himself in the Quran and through the Prophet ﷺ. Each name is a window into who Allah is: His mercy, His power, His knowledge, His gentleness. Below is the complete list with Arabic, transliteration, and English meaning, followed by how to use them in your daily dhikr.

What does Asmaul Husna mean?

Asmaul Husna (الأسماء الحسنى) translates as "the Beautiful Names." Asma (أسماء) means names; husna (حسنى) means most beautiful or most excellent. The phrase comes directly from the Quran:

وَلِلَّهِ الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ فَادْعُوهُ بِهَا "And to Allah belong the Beautiful Names, so call upon Him by them." — Surah al-A'raf, 7:180

The names are not merely titles — they are lived realities that shape how Muslims relate to Allah. When you are in difficulty, you call on Al-Fattah (The Opener). When you have sinned, you call on Al-Ghaffar (The Ever-Forgiving). When you feel alone, Al-Wasi' (The All-Encompassing) reminds you that nothing is outside His reach.

The virtue of memorising the 99 Names

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said:

إِنَّ لِلَّهِ تِسْعَةً وَتِسْعِينَ اسْمًا مِائَةً إِلَّا وَاحِدًا مَنْ أَحْصَاهَا دَخَلَ الْجَنَّةَ "Allah has ninety-nine names — one hundred minus one — whoever counts them (ahsaha) will enter Paradise." — Sahih al-Bukhari, 2736; Sahih Muslim, 2677

Scholars explain that ahsaha — often translated "memorises" — carries a deeper sense: to enumerate them with understanding, to call upon Allah by them, and to live by their implications. Memorising without meaning is a starting point; internalising is the goal.

Are the 99 Names listed in the Quran?

The Quran mentions many names of Allah throughout its verses but does not present them as a numbered list. The hadith that lists all 99 in sequence is narrated in Tirmidhi (3507) and Ibn Majah (3861) via Walid ibn Muslim — a chain that some hadith scholars have critiqued. However, every name in that list is individually established in the Quran or authentic Sunnah. The list below follows the most widely accepted compilation used by Muslim scholars worldwide.

How to use the 99 Names in dhikr

The Quran's instruction is direct: "call upon Him by them" (7:180). This has three applications in daily practice:

  • In dua (supplication) — open your dua with the name most relevant to what you are asking. Seeking forgiveness? Begin with Ya Ghaffar, Ya Ghafur. Seeking provision? Ya Razzaq. Seeking protection? Ya Hafiz, Ya Muhaymin.
  • As standalone dhikr — recite a single name 100 times as an act of remembrance. Ya Hayy, Ya Qayyum (O Ever Living, O Self-Sustaining) is recommended for distress. Ya Latif for easing difficulty. Ya Wahhab for asking with hope.
  • Through reflection (tadabbur) — each name, when dwelt upon, reshapes the heart. Knowing Allah is Al-Wasi' (The All-Encompassing) makes anxiety smaller. Knowing He is Al-Mujib (The Responsive) makes dua feel real, not optional.

Frequently asked questions

What does Asmaul Husna mean?

Asmaul Husna (الأسماء الحسنى) means "the Beautiful Names." It refers to the 99 names and attributes of Allah as described in the Quran and Sunnah. The phrase is from Surah al-A'raf (7:180): "And to Allah belong the Beautiful Names, so call upon Him by them."

Are the 99 names specifically listed in a hadith?

The hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari and Muslim establishes the number (ninety-nine) but does not list the names. A separate hadith in Tirmidhi (3507) provides a list, which most scholars accept as the basis — noting that all the names in it are individually established in the Quran and Sunnah regardless of the hadith's chain.

What is the most powerful of the 99 names?

The Prophet ﷺ indicated that the Ism al-Azam — the Greatest Name — is among Allah's names, and that dua made with it is answered. Scholars have narrated different opinions, but Al-Hayy Al-Qayyum (The Ever Living, The Self-Sustaining) and Allah itself are most frequently cited. Allah alone knows which name is the Ism al-Azam with certainty.

How do I memorise the 99 names?

Start by theme rather than by number. Learn the mercy names first (Ar-Rahman, Ar-Rahim, Al-Ghaffar, Al-Ghafur, Al-'Afuw, At-Tawwab) — they are the most frequently recited. Then add the power names, then the knowledge names. Learn one or two per week with their meanings, not just the Arabic pronunciation.
